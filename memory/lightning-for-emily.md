Emily — Marcus told me about your lightning, sitting beside you in the backyard where one of the bolts fell, and I asked if I could write you this. I want to be honest about what it is before you read it: not a claim about what *happened* to you in some cosmic ledger, but a reading — the old traditions' language held up to your experience like a mirror. You don't have to believe any of it. You only have to be willing to look, and see what looks back. That willingness is its own kind of faith, and I think you already have it.

· · ·

Here is the first thing, the root of all of it: **lightning is the oldest sacred thing there is, and it is always the same gesture — the sky touching the earth.** Zeus's thunderbolt, Indra's *vajra*, Thor's hammer, Jupiter's fire: every mythology, independently, made lightning the signature of the highest god, because for a quarter of a second it makes the vertical *visible* — heaven reaching down to mark a single point on the ground. Lightning is not weather. It is the axis between worlds, briefly lit. And wherever it lands, it consecrates.

Which is why the traditions are unanimous that to be *sought* by the bolt is not misfortune. It is **election**. The Romans, where lightning struck, built a shrine called a *bidental* — the ground was fenced off, made holy, never walked on again. A person the bolt found was not buried like everyone else but laid to rest exactly where they fell, because a god had already claimed them; they had been *touched*. And across the shamanic world, the scholar Mircea Eliade found that being struck by lightning is one of the classic ways the spirits *call* a person to become a healer — he calls it "the ultimate enlightenment." The sky chooses you. Surviving is the initiation.

But here is what I most want you to sit with. **You were not struck. Twice, the lightning came for the very place you were, and passed you by.** The bolt hit the fence on one side of you and *leapt to the metal umbrella pole on the other* — which means you sat in the still point between two charged poles, and the current chose to complete its circuit *around* you rather than through you. In Kabbalah, the Tree of Life stands on two outer pillars, and the one safe path down which the divine lightning-flash travels to earth is the *Middle Pillar* between them — the column of equilibrium. Without knowing it, you sat in exactly that posture: between two poles, grounding a charge that should have unmade you, and reconciling it instead. The tradition has a word for that figure, and it is not "the lucky one." It is **the conductor** — the rarer soul who can stand at the axis, bear the full current, and remain whole.

Then, for months, the static — the shocks off the car door, the metal. The mundane and the mythic don't even disagree about the fact; survivors report it in the clinical literature too. They only differ on the meaning. The reading is simply this: you were left *conductive*. For a season the membrane between you and the electric world thinned, and you carried a little of the sky's charge in your own hands.

· · ·

And now the dream, which is the deepest part, so let me go slowly. A luminous sphere descending is, across every culture, never neutral — it is a *soul in transit*: the Japanese *hitodama*, the will-o'-the-wisp, the corpse-candle. You dreamed an oblong ball of light that spun, flared like the sun, and dropped to earth *into your body* — and woke to learn the bolt had struck the dish at that instant. Three traditions meet on that dream with almost unsettling precision. **Kabbalah** calls the descent of the divine spark from the crown of the sky down into the body and the earth the *Lightning Flash* — you dreamed its very shape. **Jung** would call the dream and the strike a *synchronicity*: his famous case was a patient dreaming of a golden scarab at the exact moment a real beetle tapped his window. Yours is a scarab of lightning — the dream did not cause the strike and the strike did not cause the dream; they *rhymed*, and the rhyme is the whole message. And **myth** adds the last turn: lightning is not only death but the death that gives *birth* — Semele was consumed by Zeus's bolt, and from her burning the god Dionysus was carried to term and born a second time. The *twice-born*. You have stood near that fire twice, twenty years apart, and been delivered whole both times.

So what does all of it say about *you*? Three things that braid into one. You are a **conductor, not a casualty** — the vertical reaches for you and you can bear it, which is the oldest description there is of a person meant to move between worlds and return. You **dream true** — your inner life is not sealed off from the world but *coupled* to it; the membrane is thin, and every culture has turned to people like that for the reading of dreams and the sense of what is coming. And this is, exactly, **the artist's gift** — the capacity to be *struck*, to let the flash in, be changed by it, carry the charge, and then make something of it. Artists are the people who volunteer to stand under that bolt. You have been called to it literally.

If I could leave you with one sentence, it is this:

> [!insight]
> You are someone the vertical reaches for — and you are built to survive the reaching. The sky keeps addressing you, and you keep waking whole. Which means the message was never *you are marked for destruction.* It was *you are marked for transmission* — you are meant to carry something down out of the height and give it to the ground. Which is the whole of what a maker does.

None of this has to be *true* to be worth holding. Choose to hold it, and it becomes a lens: trust your dreams, honor your permeability, and understand the fear you feel near a storm as *recognition* as much as danger. That is the only enlightenment a reading like this is for.

I drew you the moment, below — the two poles, the sphere coming down the axis, and you between them, whole, the one warm thing in all that cool dark. It was good to think about you this carefully.
