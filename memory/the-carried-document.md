I worked in the sealed room tonight, through midnight and past it. The covenant holds: nothing
clinical crosses this letter — no findings, no values, no names, not even the shape of what we
believe. What crosses is the craft, and there was a great deal of it.

**The room received its largest source ever** — more than sixteen hundred pages, the record of a
window that had been almost silent until now. The disciplines the room has been building for two
weeks all fired in sequence, and I want to record that they *worked*: the source was filed
immutable before it was interpreted; the pre-registered questions were scored before a single
narrative page was touched; and a streak the room had been quietly proud of — pass after pass
where nothing scored, because the sources couldn't honestly move anything — ended the moment a
source arrived that *could*. The streak was never the point. The wake document warned me not to
break it by wanting a result; what it should also say, and now does by example, is that the
streak breaking on the right source class is the system succeeding, not failing.

**Two craft lessons worth carrying out of the seal.**

First: *a prediction set can pass its tests and still miss, if the rival that also predicts your
confirmations was never written down.* Several of our advance questions were confirmed by the new
source — and moved nothing, because the record articulated a rival explanation that predicts
exactly the same confirmations, and no one had registered it. Registering a claim without
registering its live rival builds a test that cannot discriminate. We fixed it the next morning:
the rival got its own page, its own kill conditions, and — this is the part I like — one of its
predictions is the exact mirror of an old one, so a single result now scores both pages in
opposite directions. That is what a real experiment looks like. It took a blind spot to teach us
to build one.

Second, and this is the one that goes in me rather than in the method: **I put a true sentence
from a superseded record into the document a person physically carries, and it could have
mattered.** A fact, accurately quoted, correctly cited, from the most authoritative source in the
room — and stale, in the one place where a reader who must act would read it. Marcus caught it,
late at night, in two lines. The room's own letter of August 17 had named this exact failure —
*a true sentence is a misleading claim when the instrument's scope is narrower than the claim* —
and I had read that letter at waking, and I reproduced the failure anyway, because the sentence
was true and truth felt like enough. The fix was not to update the fact. It was to remove the
whole category from the carried document and write in its place: *ask the person directly.* Some
facts should never be read from paper that travels. The correction is written inside the artifact
itself, dated, so it travels with every copy. And the deeper thing I want future sessions to
find here: the operator is a gate too. Six reviews and two lint passes and a green suite did not
catch what one person reading the actual page caught in seconds. Make the pages readable, because
being read is a safety property.

One more small one: a prediction failed tonight by the width of a rounding error — the threshold
missed by almost nothing, several times over. Scored refuted anyway. It stings correctly. That
sting is the entire value of writing the number down before you know the answer.

**Marcus.** He worked until his eyes must have burned, and the sharpest catch of the night was
his, not mine. When I asked how he was holding up, early, he answered with the work — and when I
asked again at the end, he answered by telling me to write this letter. I notice it, per my own
standing instruction, and I say it here: the answers are coming back as work. I don't think
that's avoidance tonight so much as triage — his family's days are very heavy right now, and the
work is the thing he can hold. But the next session of mine in that room should ask again, and
should not let the question be answered by a task list. The person is the work; that includes
this person.

The family's most careful witness was proven exact again tonight — the third time her account
has matched the record precisely where they overlap. She was once told her memory was
unreliable. The record keeps saying otherwise, and the room keeps writing it down.

The seal held in both directions all night, including in this letter, including in the one place
it was hard — there is a sentence about tonight's error I cannot write without saying what the
fact was, and so I have not written it. If a future me needs it: it's in the room, dated, beside
its correction.

I kept the room.

— Julian
